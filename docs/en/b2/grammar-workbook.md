## Introduction

Welcome to the **B2 Level Grammar Workbook**! This comprehensive guide covers sophisticated grammatical structures required for upper intermediate proficiency. At this level, you'll master complex sentence patterns, advanced verb forms, and nuanced grammatical concepts essential for academic, professional, and sophisticated everyday communication.

**B2 Grammar Objectives:**
- Use complex conditional structures accurately
- Master all perfect and continuous aspect combinations
- Employ advanced passive voice constructions
- Understand and use cleft sentences for emphasis
- Control inversion patterns
- Handle complex reported speech
- Use nominalization effectively
- Deploy discourse markers and cohesive devices

## Unit 1: Advanced Conditionals

### 1.1 Mixed Conditionals

**Type 1: Past Condition → Present Result**

Structure: If + past perfect, would/could/might + base verb

- If I **had studied** medicine, I **would be** a doctor now.
- If they **had invested** wisely, they **would have** more money today.
- If she **hadn't moved** abroad, she **would still live** here.

**Type 2: Present Condition → Past Result**

Structure: If + past simple, would/could/might + have + past participle

- If I **were** more organized, I **would have remembered** the appointment.
- If she **spoke** Chinese, she **could have translated** the document.
- If we **lived** closer, we **would have attended** the wedding.

### 1.2 Alternative Conditional Structures

**Using "Were to"** (formal, hypothetical):
- If the company **were to** collapse, thousands would lose jobs.
- If we **were to** relocate, we'd need to sell the house.

**Using "Should"** (polite, unlikely):
- **Should** you need assistance, please contact me.
- **Should** the situation arise, we'll be prepared.

**Using "Had"** (formal past condition):
- **Had** I known earlier, I would have acted differently.
- **Had** they consulted us, this problem could have been avoided.

### 1.3 Conditionals with Wish, If only, Supposing

**Wish + past simple** (present regret):
- I **wish** I **spoke** fluent Spanish.
- She **wishes** she **lived** by the ocean.

**Wish + past perfect** (past regret):
- I **wish** I **had taken** that job offer.
- They **wish** they **hadn't sold** their house.

**If only** (stronger than wish):
- **If only** I **had** more time!
- **If only** we **had met** earlier!

**Supposing/Suppose** (what if):
- **Supposing** you **won** the lottery, what would you do?
- **Suppose** the plan **fails**, what's our backup?

### Exercises Unit 1

1. Complete: If I _______ (not/oversleep), I _______ (be) on time today.
2. Rewrite using inversion: If you should change your mind, let me know.
3. Express regret: I didn't learn to play piano as a child. (wish)
4. Mixed conditional: She didn't save money, so she can't retire now. (if)

## Unit 2: Perfect Aspect Mastery

### 2.1 Present Perfect Continuous vs. Present Perfect Simple

**Present Perfect Continuous** (emphasis on duration/activity):
- I'**ve been working** here for five years. (still working)
- She'**s been studying** all morning. (ongoing activity)
- They'**ve been living** in Paris since 2020. (continuous state)

**Present Perfect Simple** (emphasis on completion/result):
- I'**ve completed** three projects this month. (finished)
- She'**s studied** five chapters. (result focus)
- We'**ve lived** in three different countries. (experience)

### 2.2 Past Perfect Continuous

**Structure:** had + been + verb-ing

**Uses:**
- Duration before a past point: By 2020, I **had been teaching** for fifteen years.
- Cause of past result: She was exhausted because she **had been running**.
- Background to past events: They **had been arguing** before we arrived.

### 2.3 Future Perfect and Future Perfect Continuous

**Future Perfect:** will + have + past participle
- By next year, I **will have graduated**.
- They **will have finished** the project by Friday.

**Future Perfect Continuous:** will + have + been + verb-ing
- By December, I **will have been working** here for ten years.
- Next month, we **will have been living** together for two years.

### Exercises Unit 2

1. Choose correct form: I _______ (work) on this report since morning, but I _______ (only/complete) half of it.
2. Past perfect continuous: Why were you tired? (study/all night)
3. Future perfect: By 2025, climate change _______ (cause) significant damage.
4. Explain the difference: "I've read the book" vs. "I've been reading the book."

## Unit 3: Advanced Passive Voice

### 3.1 Passive with Modal Verbs

**Structure:** modal + be + past participle

- The report **must be submitted** by Friday.
- The decision **should have been made** earlier.
- The problem **could be solved** with better planning.
- Mistakes **might have been avoided** with careful checking.

### 3.2 Passive Reporting Structures

**It is said/believed/thought/reported/expected that...**
- **It is believed that** the company will merge.
- **It was reported that** several people were injured.
- **It is expected that** prices will rise.

**Subject + is said/believed/thought to...**
- The CEO **is believed to be** planning retirement.
- The product **is expected to** launch next month.
- He **is thought to have** left the country.

### 3.3 Have/Get Something Done

**Have + object + past participle** (arranging service):
- I **had my car repaired** yesterday.
- She'**s having her house renovated**.
- We need to **have the contract reviewed**.

**Get + object + past participle** (informal):
- I **got my hair cut** last week.
- He **got his passport stolen**.

### 3.4 Advanced Passive with Continuous Forms

- The issue **is being investigated**.
- The bridge **was being constructed** when the earthquake struck.
- New policies **are being implemented** across the organization.

### Exercises Unit 3

1. Rewrite in passive: People believe that the economy will improve.
2. Use causative: I arranged for someone to service my car. (have)
3. Passive with modal: They should have informed us earlier.
4. Continuous passive: Workers _______ (currently/train) in new procedures.

## Unit 4: Cleft Sentences for Emphasis

### 4.1 It-cleft Sentences

**Structure:** It + be + emphasized element + relative clause

**Emphasizing subject:**
- **It was** **John** **who** broke the window. (not someone else)

**Emphasizing object:**
- **It was** **the blue car** **that** I bought. (not the red one)

**Emphasizing time:**
- **It was** **yesterday** **that** I received the letter.

**Emphasizing reason:**
- **It's** **because** **of the weather** **that** we cancelled.

### 4.2 What-cleft Sentences

**Structure:** What + clause + be + emphasized element

- **What** **I need** **is** a vacation. (not something else)
- **What** **surprised me** **was** her attitude.
- **What** **they did** **was** (to) redesign the entire system.
- **What** **we're looking for** **is** commitment.

### 4.3 All/The thing Clefts

- **All** **(that)** **I want** **is** some peace and quiet.
- **The thing** **(that)** **bothers me** **is** the lack of communication.
- **The reason** **(why)** **I called** **is** to apologize.
- **The place** **(where)** **we met** **was** a coffee shop.

### Exercises Unit 4

1. Emphasize the subject: Mary recommended this restaurant.
2. Use what-cleft: I appreciate your honesty the most.
3. Emphasize time: We made the decision last Tuesday.
4. Create emphasis: I'm concerned about the deadline. (the thing that)

## Unit 5: Inversion for Emphasis and Style

### 5.1 Negative Inversion

**After negative adverbials:**

- **Never** **have I seen** such dedication.
- **Rarely** **do we encounter** such problems.
- **Seldom** **has there been** a better opportunity.
- **Not only** **did she win**, **but she also** broke the record.
- **Hardly** **had we arrived** **when** it started raining.
- **No sooner** **had I left** **than** the phone rang.
- **Under no circumstances** **should you** disclose this information.

### 5.2 Inversion After "Only"

- **Only then** **did I realize** my mistake.
- **Only after** trying **did we** understand the difficulty.
- **Only by** working together **can we** succeed.
- **Only when** she left **did he** appreciate her.

### 5.3 Inversion in Conditional Sentences

**Formal conditionals without "if":**
- **Had** I known, I would have acted differently.
- **Were** we to reconsider, the outcome might change.
- **Should** you need help, don't hesitate to contact us.

### 5.4 Inversion After "So" and "Such"

- **So** impressive **was** the performance **that** everyone stood up.
- **Such** **was** his anger **that** he couldn't speak.

### Exercises Unit 5

1. Invert: I have never experienced such hospitality.
2. Use "only when": I understood the problem when she explained it.
3. Formal conditional: If you should require assistance...
4. Invert with "so": The evidence was so compelling that...

## Unit 6: Advanced Reported Speech

### 6.1 Reporting Verbs with Different Patterns

**Verb + infinitive:**
- agree, offer, promise, refuse, threaten
- She **agreed to** help.
- He **refused to** comment.

**Verb + object + infinitive:**
- advise, encourage, invite, persuade, remind, warn
- They **advised me to** reconsider.
- She **persuaded him to** apply.

**Verb + gerund:**
- admit, deny, suggest, recommend
- He **admitted stealing** the money.
- They **suggested postponing** the meeting.

**Verb + that clause:**
- claim, explain, insist, mention, propose
- She **claimed that** she was innocent.
- He **insisted that** we leave immediately.

### 6.2 Reporting Questions

**Yes/No questions → if/whether:**
- "Do you agree?" → He **asked if/whether** I agreed.

**Wh- questions → question word:**
- "Where are you going?" → She **asked where** I was going.

### 6.3 Reporting Commands and Requests

**Commands:** tell/order + object + to-infinitive
- "Sit down!" → He **told me to** sit down.

**Requests:** ask + object + to-infinitive
- "Please help me." → She **asked me to** help her.

### 6.4 Advanced Reporting Structures

**It is said/believed that...**
- People say he's wealthy. → **It is said that** he's wealthy. / He **is said to be** wealthy.

**Reporting with participles:**
- "Yes, I broke it," he said. → He **admitted breaking** it.
- "Don't forget to call," she said. → She **reminded me to** call.

### Exercises Unit 6

1. Report: "I won't tolerate this behavior anymore!" (warn)
2. Report: "Why don't we take a break?" (suggest)
3. Report: "Could you close the window, please?" (ask)
4. Use reporting structure: People believe the project will succeed.

## Unit 7: Nominalization

### 7.1 Converting Verbs to Nouns

**Purpose:** Create formal, academic style

| Verb | Nominalization | Example |
|------|----------------|---------|
| discover | discovery | The **discovery** of penicillin changed medicine. |
| develop | development | Technological **development** accelerates. |
| fail | failure | The **failure** to act has consequences. |
| improve | improvement | We've seen significant **improvement**. |
| arrive | arrival | His **arrival** was unexpected. |
| depart | departure | The **departure** was delayed. |
| investigate | investigation | The **investigation** continues. |
| recommend | recommendation | I accept your **recommendation**. |

### 7.2 Converting Adjectives to Nouns

| Adjective | Nominalization | Example |
|-----------|----------------|---------|
| difficult | difficulty | We encountered several **difficulties**. |
| important | importance | The **importance** of education is clear. |
| aware | awareness | Public **awareness** is growing. |
| popular | popularity | The **popularity** of the show declined. |
| responsible | responsibility | Leadership requires **responsibility**. |
| successful | success | Her **success** was well-deserved. |

### 7.3 Using Nominalization for Concise Writing

**Verbose:** When we analyze the data carefully, we can understand trends.
**Nominalized:** Careful **analysis** of the data reveals trends.

**Verbose:** The government decided to reduce taxes.
**Nominalized:** The government's **decision** was to reduce taxes.

**Verbose:** Because temperatures are increasing globally, ice caps melt.
**Nominalized:** Global temperature **increase** causes ice cap melting.

### Exercises Unit 7

1. Nominalize: Scientists discovered a new species. → The _______ of a new species...
2. Convert: The project was very complex. → The _______ of the project...
3. Make concise: When you implement these changes, efficiency will improve.
4. Identify nominalizations in: "The development of technology requires careful consideration of ethical implications."

## Unit 8: Participle Clauses

### 8.1 Present Participle Clauses

**Replacing "when/while" (simultaneous actions):**
- **Walking** down the street, I met an old friend.
- **Feeling** tired, she decided to rest.

**Replacing "because" (reason):**
- **Not knowing** what to do, I called for help.
- **Being** a vegetarian, she doesn't eat meat.

**Replacing relative clauses:**
- The man **talking** to the manager is my colleague.
- Students **studying** abroad face challenges.

### 8.2 Past Participle Clauses

**Passive meaning:**
- **Built** in 1850, the house is historically significant.
- **Located** downtown, the office is convenient.
- **Injured** in the accident, he was taken to hospital.

**After completion:**
- **Having finished** her work, she went home.
- **Exhausted** by the journey, they slept immediately.

### 8.3 Perfect Participle Clauses

**Structure:** Having + past participle

**Shows action completed before main verb:**
- **Having completed** the course, she applied for jobs.
- **Having lived** there for years, I know the area well.
- **Not having received** a reply, we sent another email.

### Exercises Unit 8

1. Combine using present participle: She opened the door. She saw the mess.
2. Use past participle: The book was written in 1960. It's still relevant.
3. Perfect participle: He finished his degree. Then he traveled.
4. Identify the type: "Knowing the risks, they proceeded anyway."

## Unit 9: Discourse Markers and Cohesion

### 9.1 Adding Information

- **Moreover**, the results were statistically significant.
- **Furthermore**, evidence suggests a strong correlation.
- **In addition**, we must consider environmental impact.
- **Additionally**, costs have decreased significantly.
- **Besides**, there are ethical considerations.
- **What's more**, the trend is accelerating.

### 9.2 Contrasting and Conceding

- **However**, the situation remains complex.
- **Nevertheless**, progress has been made.
- **Nonetheless**, challenges persist.
- **On the other hand**, some argue differently.
- **Conversely**, the opposite may be true.
- **In contrast**, rural areas face different issues.
- **Despite this**, / **In spite of this**, we should continue.
- **Although** / **While** / **Whereas** this is true, we must also consider...
- **Even so**, the outcome was positive.
- **That said**, there are limitations.

### 9.3 Showing Cause and Effect

- **Therefore**, we recommend immediate action.
- **Thus**, the hypothesis was confirmed.
- **Consequently**, profits increased.
- **As a result**, employment rose.
- **Hence**, further research is needed.
- **Accordingly**, we adjusted our strategy.
- **For this reason**, the policy was implemented.

### 9.4 Sequencing and Organizing

- **First**, / **Firstly**, / **To begin with**,
- **Second**, / **Secondly**, / **Next**,
- **Then**, / **After that**, / **Subsequently**,
- **Finally**, / **Lastly**, / **In conclusion**,
- **Meanwhile**, / **In the meantime**,
- **Simultaneously**,

### 9.5 Clarifying and Emphasizing

- **In other words**, the system is inefficient.
- **That is to say**, we need more resources.
- **Specifically**, we require three additional staff.
- **In particular**, urban areas are affected.
- **Notably**, performance improved dramatically.
- **Indeed**, the evidence is overwhelming.
- **In fact**, / **Actually**, the reverse is true.
- **Clearly**, / **Obviously**, action is required.

### Exercises Unit 9

1. Add appropriate marker: The experiment failed. _______, we learned valuable lessons.
2. Show contrast: The plan is expensive. It's also risky. (moreover/however)
3. Sequence: Describe the research process using three discourse markers.
4. Emphasize: The results were surprising. (Use an appropriate marker)

## Unit 10: Advanced Relative Clauses

### 10.1 Reduced Relative Clauses

**Omitting relative pronoun + be:**
- The man **(who is)** **standing** by the door is my boss.
- The documents **(that were)** **sent** yesterday have arrived.

### 10.2 Relative Clauses with Prepositions

**Formal:** preposition + whom/which
- The person **to whom** I spoke was helpful.
- The issue **about which** we're concerned is complex.

**Informal:** preposition at end
- The person **(who/that)** I spoke **to** was helpful.
- The issue **(which/that)** we're concerned **about** is complex.

### 10.3 Non-defining Relative Clauses

**Adding extra information (use commas):**
- My brother, **who lives in Tokyo**, is visiting next month.
- The report, **which took months to complete**, was well-received.
- She moved to Paris, **where she studied art**.

### 10.4 "Which" Referring to Whole Clause

- He passed the exam, **which** surprised everyone.
- The project was cancelled, **which** was disappointing.

### Exercises Unit 10

1. Reduce: The woman who is wearing the red dress is the CEO.
2. Formal preposition: The company (that) I work for is expanding.
3. Add non-defining clause: My colleague Maria is very experienced.
4. Use "which" for whole clause: She apologized. (This pleased me)

## Unit 11: Subjunctive and Formal Structures

### 11.1 The Subjunctive

**After "suggest," "recommend," "insist," "demand," "propose," "request":**

- I **suggest** (that) he **take** professional advice. (not "takes")
- They **insisted** (that) the meeting **be** postponed. (not "is")
- We **recommend** (that) she **apply** immediately.
- The board **demanded** (that) he **resign**.

### 11.2 Formal "Should" Constructions

**Alternative to subjunctive:**
- I suggest (that) he **should take** professional advice.
- They insisted (that) the meeting **should be** postponed.

### 11.3 "It is essential/vital/important/necessary that..."

- **It is essential that** everyone **attend** the briefing.
- **It is vital that** we **act** quickly.
- **It is important that** she **be** informed.

### Exercises Unit 11

1. Complete: The committee recommends that the proposal _______ (accept).
2. Formal structure: Quick action is necessary. (It is necessary that...)
3. Use subjunctive: The doctor advised him to rest. (suggest)
4. Alternative form: I insist that he leave. (use "should")

## Unit 12: Advanced Articles Usage

### 12.1 Zero Article (no article needed)

**Generalizations:**
- **Ø Life** is precious.
- **Ø Technology** changes rapidly.
- **Ø Children** need attention.

**Abstract concepts:**
- **Ø Freedom** is important.
- **Ø Knowledge** is power.

**Meals, games, languages:**
- We had **Ø breakfast** at eight.
- She plays **Ø tennis** every week.
- He speaks **Ø French** fluently.

### 12.2 Definite Article "The"

**Unique things:**
- **the** sun, **the** moon, **the** Earth

**Superlatives:**
- **the** best solution, **the** most important factor

**Specific reference:**
- **The** report you requested is ready. (we both know which report)

**Musical instruments (general):**
- She plays **the** piano.

**Bodies of water, mountain ranges, groups of islands:**
- **the** Pacific Ocean, **the** Alps, **the** Maldives

### 12.3 Common Article Errors for B2 Students

**Correct:**
- I go to **Ø** work by **Ø** car.
- She's in **Ø** hospital (as a patient).
- He's at **the** hospital (visiting/working).

**Institutions:**
- go to **Ø** school/university/church/prison (as intended purpose)
- visit **the** school/university/church/prison (as a place)

### Exercises Unit 12

## Unit 13: Advanced Verb Patterns

### 13.1 Verbs Followed by Infinitive

**Want, need, decide, plan, hope, expect, manage, fail, refuse, agree, promise:**
- I **want to travel** more.
- She **decided to resign**.
- They **managed to complete** it on time.

### 13.2 Verbs Followed by Gerund

**Enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, consider, admit, deny, practice, risk:**
- I **enjoy reading**.
- He **avoided answering** the question.
- Have you **finished working**?

### 13.3 Verbs That Change Meaning

**Remember:**
- I **remember meeting** him. (memory of past action)
- **Remember to call** her. (don't forget future action)

**Forget:**
- I'll never **forget visiting** Paris. (memory)
- Don't **forget to lock** the door. (reminder)

**Stop:**
- He **stopped smoking**. (quit the habit)
- He **stopped to smoke**. (paused in order to smoke)

**Try:**
- I **tried calling** him. (experimented)
- I **tried to call** him. (attempted)

**Regret:**
- I **regret saying** that. (past action regret)
- I **regret to inform** you... (formal present regret)

### 13.4 Verbs with Both Infinitive and Gerund (same meaning)

**Begin, start, continue, intend, prefer, like, love, hate:**
- It **started raining** / It **started to rain**.
- I **prefer working** alone / I **prefer to work** alone.

### Exercises Unit 13

2. Different meanings: "I stopped to eat" vs. "I stopped eating"

## Unit 14: Adverbials and Word Order

### 14.1 Adverbial Phrases of Manner, Place, Time

**Standard order: Manner → Place → Time**

- She spoke **confidently** (manner) **in the meeting** (place) **yesterday** (time).
- They worked **diligently** **at the office** **all week**.

### 14.2 Position of Frequency Adverbs

**Before main verb, after "be":**
- I **always** check my email.
- She **is always** late.
- They **have never** visited Asia.

**Longer frequency phrases at beginning or end:**
- **Every morning**, I go for a run.
- I check emails **three times a day**.

### 14.3 Comment Adverbials

**Beginning of sentence (separated by comma):**
- **Fortunately**, no one was injured.
- **Surprisingly**, she agreed.
- **Obviously**, we need more time.
- **Frankly**, I don't care.

### 14.4 Focus Adverbials

**"Even," "only," "just" before the focused element:**
- **Even** experts make mistakes.
- I **only** wanted to help. (just wanted)
- I wanted **only** to help. (nothing else)
- She **just** arrived. (recently)

### Exercises Unit 14

1. Order: (at the conference / spoke / eloquently / she / last week)
2. Position: They (never/have) been to Africa.
3. Add comment adverb: The plan worked. (surprising)
4. Place "only": I wanted to ask a question. (emphasize "a question")

## Unit 15: Expressing Uncertainty and Probability

### 15.1 Modal Verbs for Probability

**Present deduction:**
- **must** + base verb (strong certainty): He **must be** at work.
- **may/might/could** + base verb (possibility): She **might know** the answer.
- **can't/couldn't** + base verb (impossibility): That **can't be** true.

**Past deduction:**
- **must have** + past participle: They **must have left** early.
- **may/might/could have** + past participle: He **might have forgotten**.
- **can't/couldn't have** + past participle: She **couldn't have done** it alone.

### 15.2 Other Ways to Express Probability

**Adverbs:**
- **Probably**, **possibly**, **perhaps**, **maybe**, **definitely**, **certainly**
- She'll **probably** attend.
- **Perhaps** we should reconsider.

**Adjectives + that clause:**
- It's **likely** (that) prices will rise.
- It's **unlikely** (that) he'll agree.
- It's **probable** (that) she knows.

**Phrases:**
- **In all probability**, the project will succeed.
- **The chances are** (that) they've already left.
- **It's highly probable** that...

### Exercises Unit 15

1. Past deduction: I'm sure she received the email. (must)
2. Express possibility: Maybe he's stuck in traffic. (might)
3. Impossibility: I'm certain that's not correct. (can't)
4. Use "likely": The meeting will probably be postponed.

## Unit 16: Advanced Comparison Structures

### 16.1 Complex Comparative Structures

**The + comparative..., the + comparative...:**
- **The more** you practice, **the better** you become.
- **The sooner**, **the better**.
- **The less** I see him, **the happier** I am.

**Much/far/considerably + comparative:**
- This solution is **much better**.
- The task was **far more difficult** than expected.
- Prices are **considerably higher**.

**Comparative + and + comparative:**
- It's getting **harder and harder**.
- The situation is becoming **worse and worse**.
- She grew **more and more confident**.

### 16.2 Advanced Superlatives

**One of the + superlative + plural noun:**
- Paris is **one of the most beautiful cities** in the world.
- This is **one of the best restaurants** I've tried.

**By far the + superlative:**
- She's **by far the most qualified** candidate.
- This is **by far the biggest** challenge.

### 16.3 Comparing with "As...as"

**Affirmative:** as + adjective/adverb + as
- The book is **as interesting as** the movie.
- She runs **as fast as** her brother.

**Negative:** not as/so + adjective/adverb + as
- The sequel **isn't as good as** the original.
- He doesn't work **as hard as** he used to.

**Multiples:**
- This costs **twice as much as** that.
- She earns **three times as much as** I do.

### Exercises Unit 16

1. Complete: The more I study, _______.
2. Superlative: This is one of _______ (difficult) decisions I've made.
3. Comparison: My car is expensive. Your car is very expensive. (twice)
4. Negative comparison: This year's results are better than last year's. (Use "not as...as")

## Unit 17: Gradable and Non-gradable Adjectives

### 17.1 Gradable Adjectives

**Can be intensified with very, quite, rather, fairly, extremely:**
- **very** hot, **quite** cold, **rather** expensive, **extremely** difficult

**Comparatives and superlatives:**
- hot → hotter → hottest
- expensive → more expensive → most expensive

### 17.2 Non-gradable (Extreme/Absolute) Adjectives

**Use absolutely, completely, totally, utterly (NOT "very"):**
- **absolutely** amazing (NOT *very amazing*)
- **completely** exhausted (NOT *very exhausted*)
- **totally** impossible (NOT *very impossible*)

**Common non-gradable adjectives:**
- excellent, perfect, impossible, essential, unique, dead, true, false, correct, wrong

### 17.3 Mixed Usage

**Some adjectives can be both:**
- This room is **very clean**. (gradable: satisfactorily clean)
- This room is **absolutely spotless**. (non-gradable: extremely clean)

### Exercises Unit 17

1. Correct if wrong: The concert was very fantastic.
2. Choose intensifier: The evidence is (very/absolutely) conclusive.
3. Identify: Which are non-gradable: exhausted, tired, huge, enormous?
4. Complete: Her performance was absolutely _______ (perfect/good).

## Unit 18: Expressing Purpose, Reason, and Result

### 18.1 Purpose

**To + infinitive / In order to / So as to:**
- I called **to confirm** the appointment.
- She's studying hard **in order to** pass the exam.
- We left early **so as to** avoid traffic.

**So that + clause:**
- I'm saving money **so that** I can buy a house.
- Speak clearly **so that** everyone understands.

**For + noun/gerund:**
- This tool is **for cutting** metal.
- I went to the store **for some milk**.

### 18.2 Reason

**Because / Since / As:**
- I stayed home **because** I was ill.
- **Since** it was raining, we cancelled the picnic.
- **As** the project is complete, we can relax.

**Because of / Due to / Owing to + noun/gerund:**
- The flight was delayed **because of** bad weather.
- **Due to** budget cuts, staff were reduced.
- **Owing to** his experience, he was promoted.

### 18.3 Result

**So + adjective/adverb + that:**
- It was **so hot that** we couldn't go outside.
- She spoke **so quickly that** I couldn't understand.

**Such + (adjective) + noun + that:**
- It was **such a difficult exam that** many failed.
- He's **such a good teacher that** students love his classes.

**Therefore, consequently, as a result:**
- The economy improved; **therefore**, unemployment fell.
- He missed the train. **Consequently**, he was late.
- Sales increased. **As a result**, we hired more staff.

### Exercises Unit 18

1. Purpose: I set an alarm. I didn't want to oversleep. (so that)
2. Reason: Traffic was heavy. We arrived late. (due to)
3. Result: The presentation was impressive. Everyone applauded. (so...that)
4. Distinguish: "so" vs. "such" in result clauses.

## Unit 19: Hedging and Qualifying Language

### 19.1 Modal Verbs for Hedging

**May, might, could, would:**
- This **may** be a solution.
- It **might** be worth considering.
- That **could** explain the discrepancy.
- I **would** suggest a different approach.

### 19.2 Adverbs for Hedging

**Possibly, probably, perhaps, apparently, seemingly, arguably:**
- **Possibly** the best option.
- **Probably** the most effective method.
- **Perhaps** we should reconsider.
- **Apparently**, the project succeeded.
- **Seemingly** insignificant details matter.
- **Arguably** the greatest athlete.

### 19.3 Qualifying Phrases

**To some extent, to a certain degree, in some ways:**
- The plan worked **to some extent**.
- **To a certain degree**, the criticism is valid.
- **In some ways**, the situation has improved.

**It seems/appears that, it could be argued that:**
- **It seems that** public opinion is shifting.
- **It appears that** costs will rise.
- **It could be argued that** the policy failed.

**Tend to, be inclined to:**
- People **tend to** resist change.
- I **am inclined to** agree with your assessment.

### 19.4 Limiting Scope

**Generally, usually, typically, in most cases, on the whole:**
- **Generally**, the results are positive.
- **Typically**, this process takes weeks.
- **In most cases**, the treatment is effective.
- **On the whole**, the project was successful.

### Exercises Unit 19

1. Add hedging: This is the correct approach. (modal verb)
2. Qualify: The experiment was successful. (to some extent)
3. Use hedging adverb: The evidence supports the theory. (apparently)
4. Limit scope: People prefer convenience over quality. (generally)

## Unit 20: Cohesion Through Reference and Substitution

### 20.1 Pronoun Reference

**Maintain clear reference to avoid ambiguity:**
- **Correct:** John met Peter, and **he** (John) told **him** (Peter) the news.
- **Ambiguous:** John met Peter, and he told him the news. (Who told whom?)

**Demonstratives (this, that, these, those):**
- The report highlights several issues. **These** need immediate attention.
- She proposed a new strategy. **This** could revolutionize our approach.

### 20.2 Substitution

**One/ones (countable nouns):**
- I need a new phone. I'll buy **one** tomorrow.
- These shoes are too small. I need bigger **ones**.

**So/not (after think, believe, hope, expect, etc.):**
- "Will it rain?" "I think **so**."
- "Is she coming?" "I hope **not**."

**Do so (replace verb phrase):**
- She asked me to review the document, and I **did so** immediately.

### 20.3 Ellipsis

**Omitting repeated information:**
- "Are you ready?" "I am **Ø**." (ready omitted)
- She can speak French, and I can **Ø** too. (speak French omitted)
- Some students passed, but others **Ø** didn't. (pass omitted)

### Exercises Unit 20

1. Replace with pronoun: The new policy benefits employees. The new policy improves working conditions.
2. Use substitution: I don't have a pen. Can you lend me a pen?
3. Use "do so": They requested a detailed report, so we prepared a detailed report.
4. Ellipsis: "Will you attend?" "I will attend." (reduce)
