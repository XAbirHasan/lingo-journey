# B1 Speaking Practice

**Level:** Intermediate (B1)
**Total Scenarios:** 12

This speaking practice guide provides complex conversations, debates, and discussion scenarios for intermediate learners. Each scenario includes vocabulary, useful expressions, sample dialogues, and discussion questions to help you develop fluency and confidence.

## Scenario 1: Job Interview

### Context
You're interviewing for a position as a marketing assistant at a technology company. You need to discuss your experience, skills, and career goals professionally.

### Key Vocabulary
- qualifications, experience, responsibilities
- team player, initiative, deadline
- strengths, weaknesses, achievements
- career goals, professional development
- challenging, rewarding, opportunity

### Useful Expressions
- "I have experience in..."
- "My main responsibilities included..."
- "I consider myself to be..."
- "One of my greatest achievements was..."
- "I'm particularly interested in..."
- "What appeals to me about this role is..."
- "I believe I would be a good fit because..."
- "In my previous position, I..."

### Sample Dialogue

**Interviewer:** Thank you for coming in today. Can you tell me about your previous experience in marketing?

**Candidate:** Certainly. I have three years of experience in digital marketing. In my previous position at ABC Company, my main responsibilities included managing social media campaigns, creating content for our blog, and analyzing website traffic data.

**Interviewer:** That's impressive. What would you say is your greatest strength?

**Candidate:** I'd say my greatest strength is my ability to adapt to new situations quickly. For instance, when our company decided to shift focus from traditional to digital marketing, I took the initiative to complete several online courses and became the go-to person for social media strategy.

**Interviewer:** Can you describe a challenging project you've worked on?

**Candidate:** Absolutely. Last year, we launched a new product with a very tight deadline. I had to coordinate with the design team, write all the promotional materials, and manage the advertising budget simultaneously. Despite the pressure, we launched on time and exceeded our sales targets by 25%.

**Interviewer:** Excellent. Where do you see yourself in five years?

**Candidate:** In five years, I hope to have developed my skills in data analytics and possibly moved into a marketing manager role. What appeals to me about this position is the opportunity for professional development that your company offers.

**Interviewer:** Do you have any questions for us?

**Candidate:** Yes, could you tell me more about the team I'd be working with and what a typical day might look like in this role?

### Discussion Questions

1. What are the most important qualities employers look for in candidates?
2. How should you prepare for a job interview?
3. What questions should you ask the interviewer?
4. How do you handle difficult interview questions?
5. What are common mistakes people make in interviews?
6. How important is body language in an interview?
7. Should you discuss salary in a first interview?
8. How can you stand out from other candidates?

### Role-Play Activity

**Partner A:** You're the interviewer for a software company looking for a customer service representative.
**Partner B:** You're the candidate with two years of retail experience but no tech background.

Conduct a 10-minute interview covering:
- Work experience and transferable skills
- How to handle difficult customers
- Why you want to work in the tech industry
- Questions about the role and company

## Scenario 2: Expressing and Defending Opinions

### Context
You're participating in a group discussion about whether social media has a positive or negative impact on society. You need to express your opinion clearly and support it with reasons and examples.

### Key Vocabulary
- pros and cons, advantages, disadvantages
- argue, claim, maintain, believe
- evidence, statistics, research
- on the one hand... on the other hand
- impact, influence, effect
- privacy, connection, mental health

### Useful Expressions

**Stating opinions:**
- "In my opinion..."
- "I believe/think that..."
- "From my perspective..."
- "It seems to me that..."
- "I'm convinced that..."

**Agreeing:**
- "I completely agree with..."
- "That's exactly how I feel."
- "You make a valid point."
- "I couldn't agree more."

**Disagreeing politely:**
- "I see your point, but..."
- "I understand what you're saying, however..."
- "I respectfully disagree because..."
- "That's one way of looking at it, but..."

**Giving reasons:**
- "The reason I say this is..."
- "This is supported by..."
- "For example..."
- "Evidence suggests that..."

### Sample Dialogue

**Maria:** I believe social media has had a largely negative impact on society. It's created a culture where people are constantly comparing themselves to others, which has led to increased anxiety and depression, especially among young people.

**Tom:** I see your point, but I think you're focusing only on the negatives. Social media has connected people across the world and given voice to marginalized communities who didn't have platforms before.

**Sarah:** You both make valid points. I think the truth is somewhere in the middle. On the one hand, social media has democratized information and created opportunities for connection. On the other hand, it has raised serious concerns about privacy and mental health.

**Maria:** That's fair, but the evidence is quite concerning. Recent studies have shown that teenagers who spend more than three hours a day on social media are twice as likely to experience mental health issues.

**Tom:** I understand what you're saying, however, correlation doesn't equal causation. Perhaps young people who are already struggling with mental health issues tend to use social media more, not the other way around.

**Sarah:** Both perspectives have merit. I'm convinced that the key issue isn't social media itself, but how we use it. The reason I say this is that any tool can be beneficial or harmful depending on usage. For example, a knife can be used to prepare food or as a weapon.

**Maria:** That's an interesting analogy, but I respectfully disagree because social media platforms are specifically designed to be addictive. They use algorithms to keep users engaged for as long as possible.

**Tom:** You make a valid point about algorithms. Perhaps the solution isn't to abandon social media but to regulate how these platforms operate and educate users about healthy usage.

### Discussion Topics

1. **Technology in schools:** Should students be allowed to use smartphones in class?
2. **Remote work:** Is working from home better than working in an office?
3. **Climate change:** Should governments prioritize environmental protection over economic growth?
4. **Animal testing:** Is it acceptable to test medical products on animals?
5. **Public transportation:** Should cities ban private cars in city centers?

### Debate Structure

**Step 1:** Choose a controversial topic
**Step 2:** Divide into two teams (for and against)
**Step 3:** Each team prepares arguments (10 minutes)
**Step 4:** Opening statements (2 minutes each)
**Step 5:** Rebuttals (3 minutes each)
**Step 6:** Questions from the audience
**Step 7:** Closing statements (1 minute each)

## Scenario 3: Making Complaints

### Context
You've had several problems with a product or service and need to make a formal complaint. You must remain polite but firm while explaining the issues and requesting a resolution.

### Key Vocabulary
- dissatisfied, disappointed, unacceptable
- defective, faulty, damaged
- refund, replacement, compensation
- resolve, rectify, address
- inconvenience, frustration
- customer service, manager

### Useful Expressions

**Explaining the problem:**
- "I'm calling/writing to complain about..."
- "I'm afraid I have a complaint to make."
- "I'm not satisfied with..."
- "I'd like to bring to your attention..."

**Describing issues:**
- "The product is defective/faulty."
- "This is the third time this has happened."
- "I was promised... but..."
- "Not only... but also..."

**Requesting action:**
- "I'd like a full refund."
- "I expect this to be resolved immediately."
- "What can you do to rectify this situation?"
- "I'd appreciate it if you could..."

**Remaining firm:**
- "I'm afraid that's not acceptable."
- "I must insist on..."
- "I expect better service than this."
- "If this isn't resolved, I'll have to..."

### Sample Dialogue 1: Restaurant Complaint

**Customer:** Excuse me, I need to speak to the manager, please.

**Manager:** I'm the manager. How can I help you?

**Customer:** I'm afraid I have a serious complaint. Not only was our table not ready despite our reservation, but we've also been waiting 45 minutes for our food. This is completely unacceptable.

**Manager:** I sincerely apologize for the delay. We're short-staffed tonight, but that's no excuse. Let me check on your order immediately.

**Customer:** I appreciate that, but this isn't the first time we've had issues here. Last month, we had a similar experience.

**Manager:** I understand your frustration. To make up for this, I'd like to offer you a 50% discount on your meal today and a complimentary dessert.

**Customer:** While I appreciate the gesture, I think a full refund would be more appropriate given the circumstances. We've wasted our entire evening.

**Manager:** You're absolutely right. I'll provide a full refund and give you a voucher for a complimentary meal on your next visit. Again, I'm very sorry for the inconvenience.

**Customer:** Thank you. I hope the service improves in the future.

### Sample Dialogue 2: Product Complaint (Phone Call)

**Customer:** Hello, I'm calling to complain about a laptop I purchased from your store two weeks ago.

**Representative:** I'm sorry to hear you're having issues. Can you tell me what the problem is?

**Customer:** The laptop keeps overheating and shutting down randomly. I've tried everything suggested in the manual, but nothing works. This is clearly a defective product.

**Representative:** I apologize for the inconvenience. Have you tried updating the software?

**Customer:** Yes, I have. I must insist on either a replacement or a full refund. The product is clearly faulty, and I haven't even had it for two weeks.

**Representative:** I understand. According to our policy, you're entitled to a replacement within 30 days. I can arrange for a replacement to be sent to you tomorrow.

**Customer:** What about the inconvenience this has caused? I bought this laptop for work, and I've lost several days of productivity.

**Representative:** I completely understand your frustration. In addition to the replacement, I can offer you a £50 store credit as compensation for the inconvenience.

**Customer:** I'd prefer a discount on the replacement rather than store credit.

**Representative:** Let me speak to my supervisor. I'll call you back within the hour to confirm what we can do.

**Customer:** Please do. I expect this to be resolved quickly.

### Discussion Questions

1. How should you prepare before making a complaint?
2. What's the difference between being assertive and being aggressive?
3. When should you escalate a complaint to a manager?
4. How do cultural differences affect the way people complain?
5. What are your rights as a consumer?
6. Have you ever had to make a serious complaint? How was it handled?

### Role-Play Scenarios

**Scenario A:** You ordered a custom cake for a wedding, but it arrived in the wrong color and damaged. The bakery claims it's your fault for not being clear in your order.

**Scenario B:** Your hotel room is dirty, the air conditioning doesn't work, and you can hear loud noise from the room next door. It's midnight and you're exhausted.

**Scenario C:** You bought a phone online that was advertised as "new" but arrived with scratches and missing accessories. The seller claims all sales are final.

## Scenario 4: Giving Presentations

### Context
You need to deliver a 5-minute presentation on a topic of your choice to a group of colleagues or classmates. You must structure your talk effectively and handle questions.

### Key Vocabulary
- introduction, main points, conclusion
- audience, presentation, slides
- statistics, data, findings
- illustrate, demonstrate, highlight
- in conclusion, to summarize
- any questions?

### Useful Expressions

**Opening:**
- "Good morning/afternoon, everyone."
- "Thank you for being here today."
- "Today I'm going to talk about..."
- "My presentation is divided into three parts..."

**Structuring:**
- "First of all... Secondly... Finally..."
- "Let me start by... Then I'll move on to... And I'll finish with..."
- "This brings me to my next point..."

**Referring to visuals:**
- "As you can see from this graph..."
- "This slide shows..."
- "If you look at this chart..."

**Emphasizing:**
- "I'd like to emphasize that..."
- "The key point here is..."
- "What's particularly important is..."

**Concluding:**
- "To sum up..."
- "In conclusion..."
- "I'd like to finish by saying..."
- "Thank you for your attention. Are there any questions?"

### Sample Presentation Outline: "The Benefits of Learning a Second Language"

**Introduction (30 seconds):**
"Good afternoon, everyone. Thank you for being here. Today I'm going to talk about the benefits of learning a second language. My presentation is divided into three main parts: cognitive benefits, career advantages, and cultural understanding."

**Main Point 1 - Cognitive Benefits (90 seconds):**
"First of all, let's look at the cognitive benefits. Research has shown that bilingual people have better problem-solving skills and improved memory. As you can see from this graph, students who study a second language score higher on standardized tests. What's particularly important is that learning a language can delay the onset of dementia by up to five years."

**Main Point 2 - Career Advantages (90 seconds):**
"This brings me to my next point: career advantages. In today's globalized economy, speaking multiple languages is a valuable skill. This slide shows that bilingual employees earn, on average, 10-15% more than monolingual employees. Companies are actively seeking candidates with language skills for international positions."

**Main Point 3 - Cultural Understanding (90 seconds):**
"Finally, let's consider cultural understanding. Learning a language opens doors to different cultures and perspectives. It allows you to connect with people from around the world and appreciate literature, films, and music in their original form. I'd like to emphasize that language learning isn't just about words—it's about understanding different ways of thinking."

**Conclusion (30 seconds):**
"To sum up, learning a second language offers cognitive benefits, improves career prospects, and enhances cultural understanding. In conclusion, I believe everyone should consider learning a new language. Thank you for your attention. Are there any questions?"

### Presentation Topics

1. The impact of artificial intelligence on the job market
2. How to reduce plastic waste in daily life
3. The importance of work-life balance
4. Advantages and disadvantages of social media
5. Why everyone should learn to code
6. The future of electric vehicles
7. How to develop healthy eating habits
8. The role of arts education in schools

### Handling Questions

**If you know the answer:**
- "That's a great question. ..."
- "I'm glad you asked that..."

**If you're not sure:**
- "That's an interesting point. I don't have the exact figures, but..."
- "I'm not entirely sure, but I'll look into that and get back to you."

**If the question is off-topic:**
- "That's a valid question, but it's beyond the scope of today's presentation."
- "I'd be happy to discuss that with you after the presentation."

### Practice Activity

Prepare a 5-minute presentation on one of the topics above. Include:
- Clear introduction with structure
- 3 main points with supporting evidence
- Visual aids (graphs, images, or slides)
- Strong conclusion
- Handle 3-5 questions from the audience

## Scenario 5: Negotiating and Compromising

### Context
You need to reach an agreement with someone who has different preferences or requirements. This could be in a business context, planning an event, or resolving a disagreement.

### Key Vocabulary
- negotiate, compromise, agreement
- flexible, willing, reasonable
- concession, trade-off
- proposal, counter-offer
- meet halfway, win-win solution
- terms, conditions, deal

### Useful Expressions

**Making proposals:**
- "How about if we..."
- "What if we..."
- "I suggest that..."
- "Would you be willing to..."

**Responding to proposals:**
- "That sounds reasonable."
- "I'm afraid I can't agree to that."
- "I could go along with that if..."
- "That works for me."

**Compromising:**
- "Let's meet halfway."
- "I'm willing to... if you're willing to..."
- "Perhaps we could both make some concessions."
- "Is there any room for flexibility on..."

**Reaching agreement:**
- "So we've agreed that..."
- "Shall we shake on it?"
- "I think we have a deal."
- "Let me confirm what we've decided..."

### Sample Dialogue: Planning a Company Event

**Sarah:** Thanks for meeting with me, John. We need to finalize plans for the company retreat. I'm thinking a weekend in the mountains with team-building activities.

**John:** I understand the appeal, but I'm concerned about the budget. Mountain resorts are quite expensive, and we need to keep costs under £150 per person.

**Sarah:** I see your point. What if we looked at a location closer to the city? It wouldn't be as scenic, but it would save on transportation costs.

**John:** That's more reasonable. However, I'm also worried about timing. A full weekend might be difficult for employees with families. How about just Saturday?

**Sarah:** I'm afraid one day isn't enough for meaningful team-building. Perhaps we could compromise with Saturday and Sunday morning, ending by noon? That way, people still have Sunday afternoon with their families.

**John:** That sounds like a good compromise. Now, regarding activities, what did you have in mind?

**Sarah:** I suggest we do a mix of outdoor activities and workshops. Maybe a morning hike, followed by lunch, then afternoon workshop sessions.

**John:** I'm willing to agree to that if we can keep the workshops practical and work-related rather than abstract team-building exercises. Our employees prefer content that's directly useful to their jobs.

**Sarah:** Fair enough. What if we focused the workshops on communication skills and project management? Those are practical and applicable.

**John:** That works for me. Let's talk about catering. I know you wanted a fancy sit-down dinner, but that's where costs really add up.

**Sarah:** I could go along with a buffet-style meal if we can upgrade the quality of the food. No one enjoys cheap sandwiches.

**John:** Agreed. Let's meet halfway—a quality buffet with some premium options. I think we have a deal. So, to confirm: one and a half days, location within an hour of the city, mix of outdoor activities and practical workshops, and quality buffet catering, all within £150 per person.

**Sarah:** Perfect. I'll start looking at venues that fit these criteria and get back to you by Friday.

### Negotiation Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Salary Negotiation**
You've been offered a job at £35,000, but you were hoping for £40,000. The employer has indicated some flexibility but can't meet your full request.

**Scenario 2: Roommate Disagreement**
You and your roommate can't agree on cleaning schedules, noise levels in the evening, and having guests over. Find a compromise that works for both.

**Scenario 3: Vendor Contract**
You're negotiating with a supplier who wants a 12-month contract, but you prefer 6 months. They've quoted £5,000, which is above your £4,000 budget.

### Discussion Questions

1. What makes a successful negotiation?
2. When should you refuse to compromise?
3. How do cultural differences affect negotiation styles?
4. What's the difference between compromising and giving in?
5. How can you prepare for important negotiations?

## Scenario 6: Problem-Solving Discussions

### Context
You're working with a team to solve a complex problem. You need to analyze the situation, generate solutions, evaluate options, and reach a decision together.

### Key Vocabulary
- challenge, obstacle, issue
- solution, alternative, option
- feasible, practical, viable
- advantage, drawback, risk
- implement, execute, carry out
- outcome, consequence, result

### Useful Expressions

**Identifying problems:**
- "The main issue is..."
- "We're facing a challenge with..."
- "The problem lies in..."
- "One of the obstacles is..."

**Suggesting solutions:**
- "One possible solution would be..."
- "Have we considered..."
- "What if we..."
- "An alternative approach might be..."

**Evaluating options:**
- "The advantage of this option is..."
- "The drawback is..."
- "This seems more feasible because..."
- "We need to weigh the pros and cons."

**Making decisions:**
- "Based on our discussion..."
- "It seems like the best course of action is..."
- "Let's go with..."
- "We've decided to..."

### Sample Discussion: Declining Sales Problem

**Manager:** Thank you all for coming. As you know, our sales have dropped 15% this quarter. We need to figure out why and develop a plan to turn this around. Let's start by identifying the root causes.

**Marketing Director:** I think the main issue is that our competitors have launched similar products at lower prices. We're losing customers because of price sensitivity.

**Sales Manager:** That's part of it, but I believe the problem lies in our online presence. Our website is outdated, and our social media engagement is minimal. Customers are going elsewhere because they can't find us online.

**Product Manager:** Both valid points, but we're also facing a challenge with product quality. Customer reviews mention that our products don't last as long as they used to. We cut costs last year, and I think it's affecting quality.

**Manager:** So we have three main issues: pricing, online presence, and quality. Let's brainstorm solutions. What if we focused on improving our online marketing first?

**Marketing Director:** One possible solution would be to invest in a website redesign and hire a social media manager. This could cost around £30,000.

**Sales Manager:** That's essential, but it won't help if our prices aren't competitive. Have we considered offering promotional discounts or a loyalty program?

**Product Manager:** I'm concerned that lowering prices will hurt our profit margins even more. An alternative approach might be to emphasize quality and position ourselves as a premium brand rather than competing on price.

**Manager:** Interesting idea. What would that involve?

**Product Manager:** We'd need to improve quality first—return to better materials, which would increase costs by about 10%. Then, market ourselves as a high-quality option worth the extra money.

**Marketing Director:** The advantage of this option is that it differentiates us from competitors. The drawback is that it takes time to rebuild a quality reputation.

**Sales Manager:** We need to weigh the pros and cons. The premium strategy could work long-term, but we need short-term wins too. What if we combined approaches—improve quality for new products while running promotions on existing stock?

**Manager:** That seems more feasible. We could also invest in the website redesign to support both strategies. This seems like the best course of action. Let's develop a detailed plan with timelines and budgets.

### Problem-Solving Topics

1. **Office conflict:** Two team members aren't getting along, affecting productivity
2. **Budget cuts:** Department budget reduced by 20%; decide what to cut
3. **Client dissatisfaction:** Major client threatening to leave; how to retain them
4. **Work-from-home policy:** Develop fair policy balancing flexibility and collaboration
5. **Project delay:** Key project running two months behind; how to catch up

### Practice Framework

**Step 1:** Define the problem clearly
**Step 2:** Identify root causes (use "5 Whys" technique)
**Step 3:** Brainstorm possible solutions (no criticism at this stage)
**Step 4:** Evaluate each solution (pros/cons/feasibility)
**Step 5:** Select the best solution or combination
**Step 6:** Create action plan (who/what/when)

## Scenario 7: Describing Processes and Procedures

### Context
You need to explain how something works, describe a procedure, or teach someone how to do something complex. This requires clear, logical sequencing and precise language.

### Key Vocabulary
- procedure, process, method
- step, stage, phase
- initially, subsequently, finally
- before, after, while, during
- ensure, make sure, verify
- equipment, tools, materials

### Useful Expressions

**Sequencing:**
- "First of all..." / "To begin with..."
- "The next step is..." / "Then..."
- "After that..." / "Subsequently..."
- "Finally..." / "The last step is..."

**Explaining purpose:**
- "This is done in order to..."
- "The reason for this is..."
- "This ensures that..."

**Giving warnings:**
- "Make sure you don't..."
- "It's important to avoid..."
- "Be careful not to..."
- "Whatever you do, don't..."

**Checking understanding:**
- "Are you following so far?"
- "Does that make sense?"
- "Is this clear?"

### Sample Explanation: How to Change a Flat Tire

"Today I'm going to explain how to change a flat tire safely. Before you begin, make sure you have the necessary equipment: a spare tire, a jack, a lug wrench, and wheel wedges.

First of all, find a safe location away from traffic and apply the parking brake. Then, place wheel wedges behind the tires to prevent the car from rolling. This is essential for safety.

The next step is to remove the hubcap and slightly loosen the lug nuts—but don't remove them completely yet. This is important because it's easier to loosen them while the wheel is on the ground.

After that, place the jack under the car frame near the flat tire. Make sure you position it correctly according to your owner's manual. Then, raise the vehicle until the flat tire is about six inches off the ground.

Subsequently, remove the lug nuts completely and pull the tire towards you to remove it. Be careful, as it may be heavy. Now, mount the spare tire on the lug bolts and push it until the bolts show through the rim.

Next, put the lug nuts back on and tighten them by hand. Lower the vehicle until the tire touches the ground but doesn't bear the full weight. Then, use the wrench to tighten the lug nuts as much as possible in a star pattern—this ensures even tightening.

Finally, lower the vehicle completely and remove the jack. Give the lug nuts one last tightening to make sure they're secure. Check the pressure in the spare tire before driving.

One important warning: spare tires are temporary and shouldn't be driven faster than 50 mph. Get your flat tire repaired or replaced as soon as possible.

Are there any questions?"

### Processes to Explain

1. How to set up a home Wi-Fi network
2. How to bake bread from scratch
3. How to prepare for a natural disaster
4. How to apply for a visa
5. How your company's quality control process works
6. How to use a specific software program
7. How to plan and host a successful event
8. How photosynthesis works

### Practice Activity

Choose one of the topics above and prepare a 5-minute explanation. Include:
- Clear introduction stating what you'll explain
- Logical sequence of steps
- Warnings about common mistakes
- Check for understanding throughout
- Summary at the end

## Scenario 8: Discussing Hypothetical Situations

### Context
You're having a conversation about "what if" scenarios—imaginary situations that help explore ideas, values, and decision-making processes.

### Key Vocabulary
- hypothetical, imaginary, theoretical
- suppose, imagine, assume
- in that case, under those circumstances
- priority, choice, decision
- would, could, might

### Useful Expressions

**Setting up hypotheticals:**
- "What would you do if..."
- "Imagine that..."
- "Suppose you had to..."
- "If you could..."

**Responding:**
- "If I were in that situation..."
- "I'd probably..."
- "I think I would..."
- "That's a tough one, but..."

**Exploring implications:**
- "In that case..."
- "That would mean..."
- "The consequence would be..."

### Sample Conversation: Desert Island Scenario

**Alex:** Here's a classic question: if you were stranded on a desert island and could only bring three items, what would they be?

**Emma:** That's a tough one! I think I would bring a knife, a water purification tablet kit, and a solar-powered radio to call for help.

**Alex:** Practical choices! But imagine there was no chance of rescue—you'd be there for years. Would you change your answer?

**Emma:** In that case, I'd probably choose different items. I'd want a fishing net for food, seeds to grow vegetables, and maybe books for entertainment and knowledge.

**Tom:** I'd go for a multi-tool, a fire starter, and a large tarp. If I could survive the first few weeks, I could probably figure out the rest.

**Alex:** Interesting! What if I told you that you could bring one person with you? Who would it be and why?

**Emma:** If I had to choose, I'd bring someone with survival skills—maybe a doctor or an engineer. Someone practical who could help us stay alive.

**Tom:** I'd think about it differently. If I'm stuck there forever, I'd want someone I enjoy talking to—a best friend or family member. Survival is important, but so is mental health.

**Alex:** Good point. Now suppose you've been there for five years and a ship appears. But there's only room for one person. How would you decide who leaves?

**Emma:** That's an ethical dilemma! I suppose it would depend on who had the most to return to—like if one person had children waiting.

**Tom:** I'd probably draw straws to make it fair. Otherwise, the guilt would be overwhelming.

3. **Superpower:** If you could have one superhuman ability, what would you choose?
4. **Career:** If money weren't an issue, what job would you do?

**Dilemma 2: The Promotion**

**Dilemma 3: Found Money**

## Scenario 9: Storytelling and Narrating Experiences

### Context
You're sharing a personal experience, anecdote, or story. Good storytelling requires vivid descriptions, appropriate pacing, and emotional engagement.

### Key Vocabulary
- suddenly, unexpectedly, coincidentally
- fortunately, unfortunately
- eventually, finally, in the end
- to my surprise, to my relief
- dramatic, memorable, unforgettable
- realize, notice, discover

### Useful Expressions

**Setting the scene:**
- "It was a typical..."
- "I'll never forget the time when..."
- "This happened a few years ago..."
- "Let me tell you about..."

**Building suspense:**
- "Little did I know..."
- "What I didn't realize was..."
- "At that moment..."
- "Suddenly..."

**Reactions:**
- "I couldn't believe my eyes/ears!"
- "To my surprise/horror/delight..."
- "I was shocked/amazed/terrified..."

**Concluding:**
- "And that's how..."
- "In the end..."
- "Looking back..."
- "I learned that..."

### Sample Story: A Missed Flight

"I'll never forget the time I almost missed my flight to Japan. This happened about three years ago when I was traveling for work.

It was a typical Monday morning, and I had an 11 AM flight. I'm usually very organized, but that day, everything that could go wrong did go wrong. My alarm didn't go off, so I woke up two hours later than planned. To make matters worse, when I rushed to grab my suitcase, I realized I hadn't packed yet!

I threw things into my bag as quickly as possible and called a taxi. Unfortunately, there was an accident on the highway, and we sat in traffic for 45 minutes. I kept checking my watch, feeling more and more anxious. The driver tried to find alternative routes, but nothing was working.

When we finally arrived at the airport, it was 10:40—only 20 minutes before departure. I sprinted through the terminal, my heavy suitcase banging against my legs. At the check-in counter, the agent looked at me sympathetically and said, 'I'm sorry, but check-in closed ten minutes ago.'

My heart sank. I couldn't believe it! This was the most important meeting of my career, and I was going to miss it. I must have looked desperate because the agent picked up her phone and made a call. After what felt like forever, she smiled and said, 'You're lucky. The flight is delayed by 30 minutes due to a mechanical issue. You can still check in.'

I was so relieved I almost cried! I made it through security with minutes to spare and collapsed into my seat on the plane, exhausted but grateful.

Looking back, I learned an important lesson that day: always pack the night before, and give yourself extra time for unexpected problems. Sometimes a bit of bad luck can turn into good fortune!"

## Scenario 10: Giving Advice and Recommendations

### Context
Someone has come to you with a problem or decision, and they want your advice. You need to listen carefully, consider their situation, and provide thoughtful suggestions.

### Key Vocabulary
- advise, recommend, suggest
- situation, circumstances, dilemma
- option, alternative, possibility
- consider, think about, weigh up
- pros and cons, advantages, disadvantages
- in your shoes, in your position

### Useful Expressions

**Asking for details:**
- "Can you tell me more about...?"
- "What have you tried so far?"
- "How do you feel about...?"
- "What are your main concerns?"

**Giving advice:**
- "If I were you, I'd..."
- "Have you thought about...?"
- "My advice would be to..."
- "You might want to consider..."
- "One option would be to..."

**Being tactful:**
- "I'm not sure this is the best approach..."
- "You might want to think twice about..."
- "Have you considered the consequences of...?"

**Empathizing:**
- "I can see why you're worried."
- "That must be difficult."
- "I understand your frustration."

### Sample Conversation: Career Advice

**Student:** Thanks for meeting with me, Professor. I'm really struggling to decide what to do after graduation. I have a job offer from a big accounting firm, but I'm also considering graduate school.

**Professor:** I understand—that's a big decision. Can you tell me more about each option?

**Student:** Well, the job pays well and would give me good experience, but I'm worried I'll regret not studying further. The graduate program is in environmental policy, which I'm passionate about, but it means two more years without income.

**Professor:** Those are valid concerns. Let me ask you this: if money weren't an issue, which would you choose?

**Student:** Probably grad school. I'm really interested in environmental issues, and I think I'd enjoy the research.

**Professor:** That's telling. However, I also understand the financial reality. Have you thought about working for a year or two first, saving money, then going back to school?

**Student:** I haven't, actually. But wouldn't it be hard to return to academic life after working?

**Professor:** It can be, but many people do it successfully. In fact, you might find that work experience makes your graduate studies more meaningful. You'd have real-world context for the theories you're learning.

**Student:** That's a good point. What do you think about the accounting job specifically? It's not really related to my interests.

**Professor:** Well, if I were in your position, I'd be careful about taking a job just for the money. You spend so much time at work—it should be something you find at least somewhat fulfilling. However, having said that, any professional experience builds transferable skills.

**Student:** So you think I should go to grad school right away?

**Professor:** I'm not saying that. My advice would be to think about your long-term goals. Where do you see yourself in ten years? Then work backward from there. If environmental policy is truly your passion, you'll find a way to make it work financially.

**Student:** You're right. I think I've been too focused on the immediate decision. Maybe I should look for jobs that combine both—entry-level positions in environmental organizations where I could gain experience before grad school.

**Professor:** Now that sounds like a thoughtful approach! You might want to talk to people working in that field to learn about career paths. Informational interviews can be very helpful.

**Student:** I will. Thank you so much—this has really helped me think more clearly about my options.

### Advice Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Relationship Issues**

**Scenario 2: Money Problems**

**Scenario 3: Work-Life Balance**
A colleague is working 60-hour weeks and is stressed and exhausted, but fears their boss will think they're not committed if they set boundaries.

## Scenario 11: Discussing Abstract Topics

### Context
You're having an intellectual discussion about concepts like happiness, success, justice, or progress. These conversations require you to express complex ideas clearly and support your views with reasoning.

### Key Vocabulary
- concept, notion, idea
- define, characterize, describe
- subjective, objective
- perspective, viewpoint
- inherent, fundamental, essential
- philosophical, theoretical

### Useful Expressions

**Defining concepts:**
- "I would define... as..."
- "To me,... means..."
- "The essence of... is..."
- "... can be characterized by..."

**Expressing complexity:**
- "It's more nuanced than that."
- "There are multiple factors to consider."
- "It depends on..."
- "There's no simple answer to..."

**Philosophical discussion:**
- "One could argue that..."
- "From a philosophical perspective..."
- "This raises the question of..."
- "The fundamental issue here is..."

### Sample Discussion: What is Success?

**Interviewer:** Today we're discussing the concept of success. Let's start with a basic question: how would you define success?

**Speaker A:** That's a complex question because success is highly subjective. To me, success means achieving personal goals and finding fulfillment in what you do. It's not necessarily about money or fame.

**Speaker B:** I agree it's subjective, but I would define success more broadly. I think success is about making a positive impact on the world around you, whether that's in your community, your family, or your profession.

**Speaker C:** Interesting perspectives. I'd add that success also involves personal growth. To me, success means continuously learning and developing, not just reaching a destination.

**Interviewer:** Do you think success requires achievement, or can one be successful simply by being content?

**Speaker A:** That's a philosophical question. I believe there's a difference between success and happiness. You can be content without being successful, and successful without being content. However, ideally, success should lead to some degree of satisfaction.

**Speaker B:** I respectfully disagree. The notion that success requires constant achievement comes from our competitive society. From a different perspective, someone living a simple but meaningful life could be considered highly successful.

**Speaker C:** Both of you raise valid points. I think the fundamental issue here is that we're conflating different types of success—there's professional success, personal success, and social success. Each has different criteria.

**Interviewer:** How do you think society's definition of success has changed over time?

**Speaker A:** There's been a shift, I believe. Traditionally, success was defined by material wealth and status. Now, there's growing recognition of work-life balance, mental health, and personal fulfillment.

**Speaker B:** That's true, though I'd argue it's more nuanced than that. While some people are rejecting traditional markers of success, others still pursue them relentlessly. Perhaps what's changed is that we now have multiple competing definitions.

**Speaker C:** And social media has complicated things further. People curate images of success online, creating unrealistic expectations. This raises the question of whether we're measuring success by our own standards or by comparison to others.

**Interviewer:** Final question: Can failure be part of success?

**Speaker A:** Absolutely. I would characterize failure as a necessary component of success. Every successful person has failed multiple times. It's through failure that we learn and grow.

**Speaker B:** I'd take that further and say that our fear of failure is what often prevents success. If we redefined failure as a learning opportunity rather than a disaster, more people would take the risks necessary for success.

**Speaker C:** Well said. The essence of success, then, might not be the absence of failure but the resilience to continue despite it.

### Abstract Discussion Topics

1. **What is happiness?** Is it a fleeting emotion or a lasting state? Can it be pursued, or does it arise naturally?

2. **What makes something art?** Is art subjective, or are there objective criteria? Can anything be art if someone declares it so?

3. **What is justice?** Is justice about punishment, rehabilitation, or fairness? Can justice and mercy coexist?

4. **What is intelligence?** Is IQ the best measure? Are there multiple types of intelligence? Is emotional intelligence as important as analytical intelligence?

5. **What is progress?** Is technological advancement always progress? Should progress be measured materially or by human well-being?

6. **What is freedom?** Where does individual freedom end and social responsibility begin? Is complete freedom desirable or even possible?

7. **What is truth?** Is truth absolute or relative? Can different people have different truths? What's the relationship between truth and perspective?

8. **What is a good life?** What makes a life meaningful? Is pleasure enough, or do we need purpose? How much does morality factor into "the good life"?

### Discussion Framework

**Step 1:** Define the concept from your perspective
**Step 2:** Consider multiple viewpoints
**Step 3:** Explore contradictions and complexities
**Step 4:** Use examples to illustrate points
**Step 5:** Address counterarguments
**Step 6:** Synthesize ideas into a nuanced position

## Scenario 12: Academic and Professional Discussions

### Context
You're participating in a seminar, conference, or professional meeting where you need to discuss technical topics, research, or industry trends with colleagues and experts.

### Key Vocabulary
- research, study, investigation
- methodology, approach, framework
- findings, results, conclusions
- significant, notable, remarkable
- correlation, causation
- implication, application

### Useful Expressions

**Presenting findings:**
- "Our research indicates that..."
- "The data shows/suggests..."
- "We found a significant correlation between..."
- "The results were noteworthy because..."

**Questioning:**
- "Could you elaborate on...?"
- "How did you account for...?"
- "What implications does this have for...?"
- "Have you considered...?"

**Agreeing/disagreeing:**
- "This aligns with my own findings."
- "I'd like to challenge that assumption."
- "The evidence supports your conclusion."
- "I'm not convinced that..."

**Academic hedging:**
- "The data suggests..."
- "This appears to indicate..."
- "It's possible that..."
- "Further research is needed to..."

### Sample Discussion: Climate Research Conference

**Dr. Martinez:** Thank you for that presentation, Dr. Chen. Your research on ocean temperature changes is fascinating. Could you elaborate on the methodology you used for deep-sea measurements?

**Dr. Chen:** Certainly. We deployed autonomous underwater vehicles equipped with temperature sensors at various depths. The vehicles collected data every six hours for a three-year period, giving us approximately 4,400 data points per location.

**Dr. Johnson:** That's impressive. How did you account for seasonal variations in your analysis?

**Dr. Chen:** Good question. We normalized the data by comparing it to baseline measurements from the same months over the previous decade. This allowed us to identify trends beyond normal seasonal fluctuations.

**Dr. Martinez:** The data shows a 0.3-degree increase in average temperatures at depths below 1,000 meters. What implications does this have for marine ecosystems?

**Dr. Chen:** Our findings suggest that cold-water species may be forced to migrate to deeper or polar waters. However, I should note that we didn't study biological impacts directly—this is more of a hypothesis based on the temperature data.

**Dr. Johnson:** I'd like to challenge one of your assumptions, if I may. You've attributed the temperature increase primarily to climate change, but have you considered the possibility of localized geothermal activity?

**Dr. Chen:** That's a valid point. We did measure for geothermal vents and excluded data from those areas. However, you're right that further investigation is needed to rule out all geothermal factors completely.

**Dr. Martinez:** This aligns with my own findings in the Pacific. I observed similar patterns, though with slightly higher temperature increases. Have you considered combining our datasets for a more comprehensive analysis?

**Dr. Chen:** I'd welcome that collaboration. A larger dataset would strengthen the statistical significance of our conclusions.

**Dr. Johnson:** One more question: what are the practical applications of this research? How can policymakers use this information?

**Dr. Chen:** The evidence supports the need for stronger emissions regulations. Our data provides concrete measurements that demonstrate ocean warming is occurring even in deep waters previously thought to be insulated from surface temperature changes. This could be used to inform international climate agreements.

**Dr. Martinez:** Thank you, Dr. Chen. This has been very informative. I think we can all agree that further research is needed, but your work provides a solid foundation for understanding deep-ocean temperature trends.

### Academic/Professional Topics

1. **Technology:** The impact of artificial intelligence on employment
2. **Healthcare:** Balancing patient privacy with medical data sharing
3. **Education:** The effectiveness of online versus in-person learning
4. **Business:** Sustainable business practices in a competitive market
5. **Urban Planning:** Designing cities for climate resilience

7. **Economics:** Universal basic income—feasibility and implications
8. **Engineering:** Renewable energy infrastructure challenges

### Conference Presentation Structure

**Introduction (2 minutes):**
- State research question
- Explain significance
- Overview of methodology

**Body (5-7 minutes):**
- Present findings with data
- Show visual aids (graphs, tables)
- Discuss unexpected results
- Address limitations

**Conclusion (1-2 minutes):**
- Summarize key findings
- Discuss implications
- Suggest future research
- Thank audience

**Q&A (5 minutes):**
- Listen carefully to questions
- Acknowledge good questions
- Admit limitations honestly
- Offer to follow up if needed

- Create a research question
- Present "findings" (can be based on your knowledge/experience)
- Include at least two visual aids
- Prepare for challenging questions
- Practice academic language and hedging
