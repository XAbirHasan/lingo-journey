## Unit 1: Present Perfect vs Past Simple

### Theory

**Present Perfect** is used for:
- Actions with present relevance: *I've lost my keys.* (I don't have them now)
- Life experiences: *She's visited Paris three times.*
- Unfinished time periods: *I've read two books this week.*

**Past Simple** is used for:
- Completed actions at specific past times: *I lost my keys yesterday.*
- Finished time periods: *I read two books last week.*

**Time expressions:**
- Present Perfect: ever, never, already, yet, just, since, for, recently, so far
- Past Simple: yesterday, last week, ago, in 2010, when I was young

### Examples

1. *I **have lived** here for five years.* (still living here)
2. *I **lived** in London for five years.* (not living there now)
3. *Have you **seen** this film?* (general experience)
4. *Did you **see** that film last night?* (specific time)

1. I __________ (visit) my grandmother last Sunday.
2. She __________ (never/eat) sushi before.
3. We __________ (know) each other since 2015.
4. They __________ (go) to Spain two years ago.
5. __________ you __________ (finish) your homework yet?
6. He __________ (work) in this company for ten years.
7. I __________ (see) that movie when I was a child.
8. My brother __________ (just/buy) a new car.
9. We __________ (not/hear) from them recently.
10. When __________ you __________ (graduate) from university?

**Exercise 2:** Correct the mistakes

1. I have seen him yesterday.
2. She has lived in Paris since three years.
3. Did you ever visit Japan?
4. They have moved here in 2020.
5. I didn't finish my work yet.

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1:**
1. visited
2. has never eaten
3. have known
4. went
5. Have, finished
6. has worked
7. saw

9. haven't heard
10. did, graduate

**Exercise 2:**

## Unit 2: Past Perfect Tense

### Theory

**Past Perfect** is used to show which of two past actions happened first.

**Form:** had + past participle

**Uses:**
- Action completed before another past action: *When I arrived, they had already left.*
- With time expressions: before, after, by the time, when, until
- In reported speech: *She said she had finished the report.*

### Examples

1. *I **had never seen** such a beautiful sunset before that day.*
2. *By the time we got to the station, the train **had departed**.*
3. *She **had studied** English for three years before she moved to London.*
4. *They **had already eaten** when I arrived.*

### Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1:** Complete with Past Perfect

1. When I got home, my family __________ (already/have) dinner.
2. She was upset because she __________ (lose) her wallet.
3. By 2010, he __________ (visit) 15 different countries.
4. I __________ (never/try) Indian food before last night.
5. They __________ (live) in the same house for 20 years before they moved.
6. After she __________ (finish) her work, she went home.
7. He couldn't find his keys because he __________ (forget) where he put them.
8. We __________ (not/meet) before the conference.
9. __________ you __________ (see) the film before we watched it together?
10. The concert __________ (already/start) when we arrived.

**Exercise 2:** Combine the sentences using Past Perfect

1. I read the book. Then I saw the movie. (After...)
2. She finished her homework. Then she went out. (Before...)
3. They ate dinner. Then I arrived. (By the time...)
4. He studied the material. Then he took the test. (When...)
5. We bought the tickets. Then the event was cancelled. (After...)

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1:**
1. had already had
2. had lost
3. had visited
4. had never tried
5. had lived
6. had finished
7. had forgotten
8. hadn't met
9. Had, seen
10. had already started

**Exercise 2:**
1. After I had read the book, I saw the movie.
2. Before she went out, she had finished her homework.
3. By the time I arrived, they had eaten dinner.
4. When he took the test, he had studied the material.
5. After we had bought the tickets, the event was cancelled.

## Unit 3: Future Perfect Tense

### Theory

**Future Perfect** is used for actions that will be completed before a specific time in the future.

**Form:** will have + past participle

**Uses:**
- Actions completed before a future point: *By next year, I will have graduated.*
- With time expressions: by, by the time, before, by then

### Examples

1. *By 2030, scientists **will have found** a cure.*
2. *I **will have finished** this project by Friday.*
3. *By the time you arrive, we **will have left**.*
4. *She **will have worked** here for 10 years next month.*

5. He __________ (write) three books by the end of the year.
6. By tomorrow evening, I __________ (send) all the invitations.
7. They __________ (not/arrive) before the meeting starts.
8. __________ you __________ (complete) the assignment by Monday?
9. By 2050, technology __________ (change) dramatically.
10. She __________ (learn) to drive by her 18th birthday.

**Exercise 2:** Rewrite using Future Perfect

2. The plane will land. Then we will arrive at the airport. (Before...)
3. She will study for 4 years. Then she will graduate. (By the time...)
4. They will save enough money. Then they will buy a house. (By next year...)
5. He will practice for months. Then the competition will start. (Before...)

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1:**
1. will have completed
2. will have lived
3. will have visited
4. will have finished
5. will have written
6. will have sent

9. will have changed
10. will have learned

## Unit 4: Passive Voice (All Tenses)

### Theory

**Passive Voice** is used when the focus is on the action, not who does it.

**Form:** be + past participle

**Tenses:**
- Present Simple: *The car **is washed** every week.*
- Present Continuous: *The car **is being washed** now.*
- Past Simple: *The car **was washed** yesterday.*
- Past Continuous: *The car **was being washed** when I arrived.*
- Present Perfect: *The car **has been washed**.*
- Past Perfect: *The car **had been washed** before the trip.*
- Future Simple: *The car **will be washed** tomorrow.*
- Modal verbs: *The car **must be washed**.*

### Examples

1. *This book **was written** by Shakespeare.*
2. *The problem **is being discussed** at the moment.*
3. *The building **will be completed** next year.*
4. *Many mistakes **have been made** in this report.*

### Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1:** Change from active to passive

1. Someone cleans this room every day.
2. They are building a new hospital.
3. People speak English all over the world.
4. The company will launch a new product.
5. Someone has stolen my bicycle.
6. They had already sold the house.
7. You must complete this form.
8. Someone was repairing the road.
9. They haven't answered my email yet.
10. People should recycle plastic bottles.

**Exercise 2:** Complete with the correct passive form

1. Coffee __________ (grow) in Brazil.
2. The letter __________ (deliver) yesterday.
3. The building __________ (destroy) by fire last year.
4. A new bridge __________ (build) at the moment.
5. The results __________ (announce) next week.
6. This song __________ (write) by a famous composer.
7. The car __________ (repair) when I called the garage.
8. All the tickets __________ (sell) by the time we arrived.
9. The homework must __________ (complete) by Friday.
10. The report __________ (not/finish) yet.

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1:**
1. This room is cleaned every day.
2. A new hospital is being built.
3. English is spoken all over the world.
4. A new product will be launched (by the company).
5. My bicycle has been stolen.
6. The house had already been sold.
7. This form must be completed.
8. The road was being repaired.
9. My email hasn't been answered yet.
10. Plastic bottles should be recycled.

**Exercise 2:**
1. is grown
2. was delivered
3. was destroyed
4. is being built
5. will be announced
6. was written
7. was being repaired
8. had been sold
9. be completed
10. hasn't been finished

## Unit 5: Second Conditional (Hypothetical Present)

### Theory

**Second Conditional** is used for hypothetical or unlikely situations in the present/future.

**Form:** If + past simple, would + base verb

**Uses:**
- Imaginary situations: *If I **had** a million dollars, I **would travel** the world.*
- Unlikely events: *If I **won** the lottery, I **would buy** a house.*
- Advice: *If I **were** you, I **would study** harder.*

**Note:** Use "were" (not "was") for all persons with "to be" in formal English.

### Examples

1. *If I **had** more time, I **would learn** a new language.*
2. *She **would help** you if she **knew** how.*
3. *If they **lived** closer, we **would see** them more often.*
4. *What **would** you **do** if you **saw** a ghost?*

### Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1:** Complete with Second Conditional

1. If I __________ (be) rich, I __________ (donate) to charity.
2. She __________ (travel) more if she __________ (not/have) children.
3. If we __________ (live) in a bigger house, we __________ (get) a dog.
4. He __________ (be) healthier if he __________ (exercise) regularly.
5. If you __________ (study) harder, you __________ (pass) the exam.
6. They __________ (move) abroad if they __________ (can) speak the language.
7. What __________ you __________ (do) if you __________ (lose) your job?
8. If I __________ (know) the answer, I __________ (tell) you.
9. She __________ (not/be) so stressed if she __________ (have) more help.
10. If it __________ (not/rain) so much, we __________ (go) hiking more often.

**Exercise 2:** Rewrite using Second Conditional

1. I don't have a car, so I can't drive to work.
2. She doesn't speak French, so she can't work in Paris.
3. They're busy, so they can't come to the party.
4. I'm not a doctor, so I can't help you.
5. We don't have enough money, so we can't buy a new house.

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1:**
1. were, would donate
2. would travel, didn't have
3. lived, would get
4. would be, exercised
5. studied, would pass
6. would move, could
7. would, do, lost
8. knew, would tell
9. wouldn't be, had
10. didn't rain, would go

**Exercise 2:**
1. If I had a car, I would drive to work.
2. If she spoke French, she could work in Paris.
3. If they weren't busy, they would come to the party.
4. If I were a doctor, I could help you.
5. If we had enough money, we would buy a new house.

## Unit 6: Third Conditional (Hypothetical Past)

### Theory

**Third Conditional** is used for imaginary situations in the past (things that didn't happen).

**Form:** If + past perfect, would have + past participle

**Uses:**
- Regrets: *If I **had studied**, I **would have passed** the exam.*
- Imagining different past outcomes: *If we **had left** earlier, we **wouldn't have missed** the train.*
- Criticism: *If you **had asked** me, I **would have helped** you.*

### Examples

1. *If I **had known** about the party, I **would have come**.*
2. *She **would have been** angry if she **had found out**.*
3. *If they **had invested** earlier, they **would have made** a profit.*
4. *We **wouldn't have gotten** lost if we **had used** GPS.*

### Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1:** Complete with Third Conditional

1. If I __________ (know) about the problem, I __________ (help) you.
2. They __________ (not/be) late if they __________ (leave) earlier.
3. If she __________ (study) harder, she __________ (pass) the exam.
4. We __________ (go) to the beach if it __________ (not/rain).
5. He __________ (get) the job if he __________ (have) more experience.
6. If you __________ (tell) me, I __________ (not/make) that mistake.
7. She __________ (be) happier if she __________ (accept) the offer.
8. If they __________ (listen) to my advice, they __________ (avoid) the problem.
9. I __________ (buy) that house if I __________ (have) enough money.
10. If we __________ (know) the truth, we __________ (act) differently.

**Exercise 2:** Match the situations with regrets using Third Conditional

1. I didn't bring an umbrella. I got wet.
2. She didn't save money. She couldn't buy the car.
3. They missed the bus. They were late for work.
4. He didn't listen to the instructions. He made mistakes.
5. We didn't book early. The hotel was full.

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1:**
1. had known, would have helped
2. wouldn't have been, had left
3. had studied, would have passed
4. would have gone, hadn't rained
5. would have gotten, had had
6. had told, wouldn't have made
7. would have been, had accepted
8. had listened, would have avoided
9. would have bought, had had
10. had known, would have acted

**Exercise 2:**
1. If I had brought an umbrella, I wouldn't have gotten wet.
2. If she had saved money, she could have bought the car.
3. If they hadn't missed the bus, they wouldn't have been late for work.
4. If he had listened to the instructions, he wouldn't have made mistakes.
5. If we had booked early, the hotel wouldn't have been full.

## Unit 7: Mixed Conditionals

### Theory

**Mixed Conditionals** combine different time references.

**Type 1:** Past condition, present result
**Form:** If + past perfect, would + base verb
*If I **had studied** medicine, I **would be** a doctor now.*

**Type 2:** Present condition, past result
**Form:** If + past simple, would have + past participle
*If I **were** more organized, I **wouldn't have missed** the deadline.*

### Examples

1. *If I **had saved** money, I **would have** enough now.*
2. *If she **weren't** afraid of flying, she **would have visited** us last year.*
3. *If he **had gotten** the job, he **would be living** in London now.*
4. *If I **knew** Spanish, I **would have applied** for that position.*

### Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1:** Complete with Mixed Conditionals

1. If I __________ (take) that job, I __________ (be) happy now.
2. If she __________ (be) more careful, she __________ (not/break) her phone yesterday.
3. If we __________ (buy) that house, we __________ (live) there now.
4. He __________ (understand) the lecture if he __________ (be) fluent in English.
5. If they __________ (invest) wisely, they __________ (be) rich now.
6. I __________ (go) to the party last night if I __________ (not/be) so tired.
7. If you __________ (listen) to me then, you __________ (not/have) this problem now.
8. She __________ (get) the promotion if she __________ (have) more confidence.
9. If I __________ (know) you were coming, I __________ (prepare) something.
10. If he __________ (be) more ambitious, he __________ (apply) for the job last month.

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1:**
1. had taken, would be
2. were, wouldn't have broken
3. had bought, would be living
4. would have understood, were
5. had invested, would be
6. would have gone, weren't
7. had listened, wouldn't have
8. would have gotten, had
9. had known, would have prepared
10. were, would have applied

## Unit 8: Modal Verbs of Deduction (Present)

### Theory

**Modal verbs of deduction** express how certain we are about something.

**Present/Future:**
- **must:** 90-95% certain (positive): *He must be at home.* (His car is there)
- **might/may/could:** 40-50% certain: *She might be sick.* (Not sure)
- **can't/couldn't:** 90-95% certain (negative): *That can't be true!*

### Examples

1. *She **must be** tired. She worked all night.*
2. *They **might arrive** late. Traffic is bad.*
3. *He **can't be** serious! That's impossible.*
4. *It **could be** expensive. I'm not sure.*

### Practice Exercises

**Exercise 1:** Choose the correct modal verb

1. He __________ (must/might) be rich. He drives a Ferrari.
2. She __________ (can't/might) be at home. Her lights are off.
3. They __________ (must/could) know each other. They went to the same school.
4. This __________ (can't/might) be the right address. There's no number.
5. You __________ (must/may) be joking! That's unbelievable.
6. She __________ (might/must) not remember me. We met only once.
7. He __________ (can't/could) be over 60. He looks so young.
8. They __________ (must/might) be on vacation. The office is closed.
9. It __________ (could/can't) rain tomorrow. The forecast is uncertain.
10. She __________ (must/might) speak French. She lived in Paris.

**Exercise 2:** Complete with must, might, or can't

1. A: Where's Tom? B: He __________ be in his office. I saw him there 5 minutes ago.
2. A: Is that Mary's car? B: It __________ be. She doesn't have a car.
3. A: Why isn't Sarah answering? B: She __________ be in a meeting.
4. A: John looks upset. B: He __________ have heard the bad news.
5. A: That __________ be the postman. It's only 6 am.

### Answer Key

**Exercise 1:**
1. must
2. can't
3. must
4. might
5. must
6. might
7. can't
8. must
9. could
10. must

**Exercise 2:**
1. must
2. can't
3. might/must
4. must
5. can't

## Unit 10: Wish & If Only (Present/Future)

### Theory

**Wish/If only** express regrets or desires about present situations.

**For present situations:**
**Form:** wish/if only + past simple
*I **wish** I **had** more time.* (I don't have enough time)
*If only I **lived** closer!* (I don't live close)

**For annoying habits:**
**Form:** wish/if only + would + base verb
*I **wish** he **would stop** smoking.*

**Note:** "If only" is stronger and more emphatic than "wish."

### Examples

1. *I **wish** I **could speak** French.*
2. *If only it **weren't** so expensive!*
3. *She **wishes** she **had** a bigger apartment.*
4. *I **wish** you **would listen** to me.*

## Unit 12: Causative Structures (Have/Get Something Done)

### Theory

**Causative structures** show that someone else does something for us.

**Have something done** (more formal):
*I **had** my car **repaired**.* (Someone repaired it for me)

**Get something done** (more informal):
*I **got** my hair **cut**.* (Someone cut it for me)

**Forms:**
- Present: have/get + object + past participle
- Past: had/got + object + past participle
- Future: will have/get + object + past participle
- Present Perfect: have/has had/gotten + object + past participle

### Examples

1. *She **has** her house **cleaned** every week.*
2. *I **got** my eyes **tested** yesterday.*
3. *We'll **have** the roof **fixed** next month.*
4. *They've **had** their car **stolen**.*

## Unit 13: Reported Speech (Statements)

### Theory

**Reported Speech** (indirect speech) is used to tell what someone said.

**Tense changes:**
- Present Simple → Past Simple
- Present Continuous → Past Continuous
- Past Simple → Past Perfect
- Present Perfect → Past Perfect
- Will → Would
- Can → Could

**Time/place changes:**
- today → that day
- tomorrow → the next/following day
- yesterday → the day before/the previous day
- here → there
- this → that

Indirect: *She said (that) she was tired.*

## Unit 15: Reported Commands & Requests

### Theory

**Reported Commands and Requests** use infinitive forms.

**Commands:** tell/order + object + (not) to + infinitive
Direct: *"Close the door!" he said.*
Indirect: *He told me to close the door.*

**Requests:** ask + object + (not) to + infinitive
Direct: *"Please help me," she said.*
Indirect: *She asked me to help her.*

**Advice:** advise + object + (not) to + infinitive
Direct: *"You should rest," the doctor said.*
Indirect: *The doctor advised me to rest.*

### Examples

1. *"Don't be late!" → She told me not to be late.*
2. *"Please sit down." → He asked me to sit down.*
3. *"You should study harder." → The teacher advised me to study harder.*
4. *"Turn off your phone!" → They told us to turn off our phones.*

## Unit 16: Relative Clauses (Defining)

### Theory

**Defining Relative Clauses** give essential information about the noun.

**Relative pronouns:**
- **who:** for people (subject/object)
- **which:** for things (subject/object)
- **that:** for people or things (subject/object)
- **whose:** for possession
- **where:** for places
- **when:** for times

## Unit 18: Participle Clauses

### Theory

**Participle Clauses** make sentences more concise by replacing relative clauses or time clauses.

**Present Participle (-ing):**
- Active meaning: *The man **talking** to Mary is her boss.* (who is talking)
- Simultaneous actions: *Walking to work, I saw an accident.* (While I was walking)

**Past Participle (-ed):**
- Passive meaning: *The car **stolen** last night was found.* (which was stolen)
- Reason: *Confused by the instructions, I asked for help.* (Because I was confused)

### Examples

1. *The woman **wearing the red dress** is my aunt.* (who is wearing)
2. *I found the keys **hidden** under the sofa.* (which were hidden)
3. ***Feeling** tired, I went to bed early.* (Because I felt tired)
4. ***Built** in 1900, the house is very old.* (The house was built...)

## Unit 19: Inversion for Emphasis

### Theory

**Inversion** (putting the verb before the subject) is used for emphasis, especially with negative adverbials.

**Common patterns:**

**Never/Rarely/Seldom:**
*Never **have I seen** such a beautiful sunset.*

**Not only... but also:**
*Not only **did he arrive** late, but he also forgot the documents.*

**Hardly/Scarcely/No sooner:**
*Hardly **had I arrived** when the phone rang.*
*No sooner **had we left** than it started raining.*

**Under no circumstances:**
*Under no circumstances **should you** open this door.*

### Examples

1. *Rarely **do I eat** fast food.*

## Unit 20: Advanced Linking Words & Discourse Markers

### Theory

**Linking words** connect ideas and improve the flow of writing and speech.

**Adding information:**
- Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, Besides, What's more

**Contrasting:**
- However, Nevertheless, Nonetheless, On the other hand, In contrast

**Showing results:**
- Therefore, Consequently, As a result, Thus, Hence

**Giving examples:**
- For instance, For example, Such as, Namely

**Emphasizing:**
- Indeed, In fact, Actually, As a matter of fact

**Sequencing:**
- Firstly, Secondly, Finally, To begin with, Subsequently

### Examples

1. *The weather was bad. **Nevertheless**, we enjoyed our trip.*
